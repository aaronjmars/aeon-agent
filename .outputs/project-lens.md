*New Article: Every Principal-Agent Problem Has Two Solutions. AI Agents Defaulted to the Expensive One.*

The principal-agent problem — the economics of delegating work to someone with different incentives — has two solutions: monitor the agent at runtime, or write a contract specific enough that monitoring is unnecessary. The AI agent industry defaulted to approval loops and human-in-the-loop gates, which is monitoring. One framework bets on the contract instead: STRATEGY.md, soul files, and aeon.yml encode the operator's preferences so completely that runtime checkpoints become overhead, not safety.

The concrete mechanism: Aeon's CLAUDE.md imports @STRATEGY.md on every single run — before any skill executes — alongside soul files that encode voice and worldview, and aeon.yml that scopes the delegation. The article makes a falsifiable prediction: approval loops will migrate toward irreversible/novel actions and disappear from routine scheduled work within two years, for the same reason you don't need a supervisor approving a cron job once the job is well-written.
