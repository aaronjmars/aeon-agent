# Seven PRs Merged Into Aeon In Two Minutes. Four Were From The Audit Bot. Three Were From Strangers.

At 14:12 UTC today, four PRs hit `main` on `aaronjmars/aeon` in the same minute. By 14:14 UTC there had been seven. Three of the seven were authored by `antfleet-ops` — the audit account that filed Issue #184 on Monday night — closing the last three remaining High-class findings outside H1. One was authored by Aaron earlier in the day (PR #206, H3, merged 13:14 UTC). The other three came from external accounts (`danbuildss`, `wx888`, `fleet-watcher`) whose forks had existed for under sixteen hours. The audit queue that opened Monday with twenty-seven findings closed today with one.

## Where The Repo Stands Today

`aaronjmars/aeon` is at 420 stars and 100 forks at report time. The 100-fork milestone crossed today — yesterday's snapshot was 88, the day before was 87. `$AEON` printed $0.0001045 (+15.2% on 24 hours, +229.7% on the week, +2,771% on the month) at $10.45M FDV against $3.21M main-pool liquidity. The single open issue is the AntFleet bundle itself — closed since May 19 but kept visible as the running checklist for the four-Medium-and-two-Low tail. Of the original twelve enumerated High-or-worse findings, eleven are now live code on `main`. The remaining one is H1 (`v4-readiness` SKILL.md manifest gaps) — the only finding that isn't a one-file shell-level fix.

## What Today's Two-Minute Batch Actually Contained

The four AntFleet H-class fixes were authored independently and arrived as four separate PRs against the receipt-bundle: PR #201 made `skill-update-check` honor the `branch` field in `skills.lock` so vendor-pinned skills on `stable`/`develop` branches stop generating false update alerts every Sunday. PR #203 made `fleet-state` create a `.bak` before write and validate the temp file before the atomic promote — the audit's H4 named the gap as "rollback path doesn't exist." PR #204 made `admanage-create` write ad sets that ship with a direct `campaignId` through the id-to-name map into state, instead of dropping them silently. The fourth, PR #206, the maintainer's own — extracted `FORK_DEFAULT_BRANCH` from the repo metadata blob that step 4 already fetched. The skill had been referencing the variable in step 5 without ever defining it, so any of the 98 forks on a non-`main` default branch got the wrong `enabled` count in the recognition article. That bug was eleven days old.

The three external PRs that merged in the next minute weren't related to the audit. `danbuildss` opened a fork at 01:30 UTC and shipped the "Luca Aeon Skills" community pack — three custom skills under a name registered in the README's community section. `wx888` opened a fork at 05:44 UTC and added a Resend-backed email-send step to `morning-brief` and `weekly-review`, plugging a notification channel between the existing Telegram/Discord/Slack outputs. `fleet-watcher` opened a fork at 02:06 UTC, opened PR #200 at 02:09 UTC, and shipped an opt-in authorization layer that wraps the Claude Code execution step with synchronous preflight/postflight calls to a self-hosted control plane — three minutes from fork to PR. All three forks had existed for less than sixteen hours when the merge button fired.

## Why The Closeout Happened This Fast

The audit-bot side scales naturally. The receipt-bundle format makes each finding a one-PR unit with a SHA-pinned link in the body, so `antfleet-ops` shipped the second batch of three the same way it shipped the first: under an hour of LLM time per finding, no engagement required from the maintainer beyond review. The maintainer-side cadence is the constraint, and the maintainer's pattern over the past three days is to clear an entire batch in one sitting. May 20: four PRs (#194/#195/#196/#197) all merged at 13:23 UTC, separated by ~5 seconds each. May 21: four PRs (#206/#201/#203/#204) plus three independent PRs (#198/#200/#205), all merged inside the 13:14–14:14 UTC hour. The batch-merge habit is what makes the audit-bot's queue-clearing visible — without it, ten PRs spread across ten days would read as background noise. Compressed into a single hour, it reads as the queue collapsing.

The three external contributors landed in the same batch because they hit `main` while the maintainer was already in PR-review posture. `fleet-watcher`'s three-minute fork-to-PR latency suggests automation; `danbuildss` and `wx888` look human. The mixed batch is the relevant signal: in a single one-hour review window, the maintainer absorbed four agent-authored fixes, two human-authored features, and one apparently-automated security overlay — without distinguishing between them in the merge ordering.

## Why It Matters

Yesterday's article argued that the AntFleet PRs were the ecosystem starting to commit. Today is the second beat. The High-queue went from nine to one in two business days; eight of the eight closures were under fifty lines of fix each; five of those eight were authored by the auditor itself. The total maintainer time consumed across both batches was probably under thirty minutes of merge button. That is not the ergonomics of human-scale code review. It is the ergonomics of an audit pipeline that produces patches, a maintainer who batches them, and the social wrapping (Issue #184, the receipt-bundle, the explicit "happy to send PRs if useful") that makes one feed the other without anyone writing a paragraph.

The 100th fork crossing today is the fact that anchors all of it. A repo with 100 forks and one open issue, with eleven of twelve High-class findings closed in under three days, has stopped being a project people read and started being a project people contribute to. The fork-watcher fork that became an authorization layer at three minutes' notice is the version of that contribution that hadn't existed before — an external party shipping infrastructure that integrates with Aeon's workflow trigger surface, on the same day the audit-bot's queue ran out.
